North is up, East is left. Click anywhere in the image to see the spectrum at that spot. With the image selected, Space plays or pauses and the arrow keys step one image at a time. Each exposure sees a different wavelength, so not every flicker is a real change in brightness. “Only what changed” subtracts the typical sky, leaving movers and variables: red is brighter than usual, blue is fainter. A dot with no Gaia ring around it is not in the Gaia catalogue. Pixels the archive marked as bad are filled in from the other images for display, and the blanked-out cores of very bright stars are drawn white; measurements ignore both.

Adding exposures together gives a cleaner picture. Subtracting the early stack from the late one makes anything that moved show up as a blue (earlier) and red (later) pair.

Or click the image (or press Enter on it) to flip it by hand.

Brightness depends on wavelength. To judge real variability, compare points of similar colour.

Each SPHEREx exposure measures one wavelength at each position, so a spectrum builds up from many exposures. Click anywhere in the tracker to get the spectrum there.

One row per image. “Flags at target” are the archive’s warnings for the pixel under the tracked object; bad pixels are already blanked out. Click a row to open that image in the tracker.

    Where the data comes from, and who can use this site

    Every image in the tracker is a NASA SPHEREx observation from NASA’s public archive, either fetched when you ask or saved on this site earlier. The site is free, needs no account, and stores nothing about you.
